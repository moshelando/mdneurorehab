Programs

Rehabilitation Programs

Coordinated areas of care that may bring physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech-language therapy together around a patient’s needs.

Five principal program areas

These programs define the practice more clearly than a long undifferentiated list of diagnoses and techniques.

1

Neurological Rehabilitation

Multidisciplinary care for people whose movement, balance, communication, cognition, or independence has been affected by a neurological condition.

Learn more →
2

Brain Injury & Stroke Rehabilitation

A coordinated program addressing mobility, communication, cognition, self-care, and participation after stroke or acquired brain injury.

Learn more →
3

Balance, Gait & Fall Prevention

Evaluation and treatment for walking difficulty, dizziness, balance loss, fall risk, and reduced confidence with movement.

Learn more →
4

Pediatric Rehabilitation

Physical, occupational, speech, feeding, and developmental support for children and their families.

Learn more →
5

Pain, Orthopedic & Post-Surgical Rehabilitation

Focused rehabilitation for pain, injury, surgery recovery, and musculoskeletal limitations that affect movement and daily life.

Learn more →

What makes a program different from a therapy discipline?

A discipline—such as physical therapy—is a professional form of care. A program—such as neurological rehabilitation—organizes one or more disciplines around a category of patient need.

Example

Stroke rehabilitation may involve physical therapy for mobility, occupational therapy for daily activities, and speech-language therapy for communication, cognition, or swallowing.

Maryland Neuro-Rehab

Coordinated outpatient rehabilitation for mobility, communication, cognition, everyday function, and participation.
